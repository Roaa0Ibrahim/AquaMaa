## Slide 8: What You Are Building

One live chain, from water samples to a robot that talks about them.

1

ESP32 Sensor Nodes

Probes in water, ESP32 samples them, timestamped JSON or CSV

2

Database Storage

Every reading persisted with time, sensor ID, and value

3

Live Dashboard

Current values, units, history, refresh under 5 seconds

4

Wireless Connectivity

No USB data cable; same backend and dashboard

5

NAO or Pepper

Robot fetches live data, speaks, answers, gestures

Rule of the chain: every downstream component must use real data from the previous stage. No hard-coded values.

**Speaker notes:**

> Architecture, 60 seconds. Walk from left to right. Stress the rule at the bottom: real data must flow from one stage to the next, with no hard-coded values.

## Slide 9: Your Starting Kit

*Image: esp32_kit.jpg*

Illustrative setup (AI-generated). Your physical kit and its datasheets are the reference.

- Controller: ESP32 board, USB cable, breadboard, jumper wires, resistors
- Sensors: water level, temperature, pH, salinity, turbidity
- Water samples: tap, salty, and slightly acidic, for meaningful variation
- Workstation: one laptop per team with power; personal laptops recommended
- Connectivity: broadband device for wireless connectivity
- Robots: NAO or Pepper

First job: photograph, label, and inventory every item. Report anything missing before you code.

**Speaker notes:**

> Kit orientation, 60 seconds. Ask teams to inspect the physical kit and supplied datasheets. Clarify that the displayed setup is illustrative. The first team action is inventory, labeling, and reporting missing items.

## Slide 10: Five Milestones, in Order

| Milestone | Minimum bar (required) | Optional differentiators |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 IoT Foundation | 3+ sensors; timestamped structured data | 5 sensors, calibration, battery/solar, sensor health |
| 2 Data Storage | Store/query readings; survives restart | Time-series DB, cloud sync, data cleaning, sensor fusion |
| 3 Smart Dashboard | Live values, history, <5 s latency | Alerts, trends, anomaly detection, AI analysis, recommendations, mobile app |
| 4 Wireless + Predictive AI | Wireless data; recovery after disconnect | LoRa, link quality, forecasting, risk prediction, early warnings |
| 5 Intelligent Robot | Fetch/speak data, answer questions, gesture | Computer vision, object recognition, autonomous movement, obstacle avoidance, voice AI, proactive inspection and actions |

**Speaker notes:**

> Milestones, 75 seconds. Explain the middle column as mandatory and the right column as optional differentiation. Teams should finish the minimum bar in order before adding advanced features.

## Slide 11: Milestone 5: NAO or Pepper

*Image: pepper.jpg*

The robot is the face of your system. Minimum bar:

- Fetches current data from your own backend (not a file)
- Speaks the current readings on request
- Answers at least two distinct questions
- Performs at least one gesture or motion

Test it like a reviewer: change a sensor value, ask the robot, and hear the new value.

**Speaker notes:**

> Robot milestone, 50 seconds. The robot must query the team's live backend. Demonstrate the reviewer test: change a sensor value, ask the robot, and verify that the spoken value changes.

## Slide 14: Rules, Safety, and Support

Working rules

- Access: All participating students are granted access to the building from 2 to 16 November from 9 am to 3pm. Access in other times can be granted if needed subject to postdocs and faculty availability in the center.
- Water safety: only probe tips go in water; keep containers away from boards, PCs, power, and robots.
- Allowed: internet search, LLMs, open-source libraries. Your team must understand and explain all code.
- Equipment: hardware and robots stay in Building A25.
- Live evidence: a feature that does not run on the day does not count.

Support you can count on

- Technical guides today: short presentations by Mr. Ahmad Safari and Dr. Fursan Thabit.
- Documentation: datasheets, wiring diagrams, robot connection guide.
- When asking for help, bring: the exact error, a wiring photo, and what you already tried.

**Speaker notes:**

> Rules and support, 55 seconds. Stress water safety, equipment staying in Building A25, and live evidence. Internet search, LLMs, and open-source libraries are allowed, but teams must understand and explain their code.
