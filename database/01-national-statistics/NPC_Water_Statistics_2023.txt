Preface

                         Allah the Exalted said in Holy Quran: “We made from water every
                         living thing”*. Hence, water is life!

                         The conservation of Qatar's natural freshwater resources is
                         therefore a critical aspect of the National Development Strategy.

                         Qatar relies on seawater desalination as the primary source of
                         drinking water and on groundwater abstraction for agricultural
                         purposes. The reuse of treated wastewater has emerged as a
                         significant alternative source for irrigation in agriculture and
                         landscaping.

                         Qatar's water policies have yielded significant successes,
                         including the provision of safe drinking water for all population,
                         minimum water loss, high-level treatment of urban wastewater
                         and re-use of substantial amounts of treated wastewater.
                         Statistics show that water use efficiency has increased in most
                         economic sectors.

                         However, freshwater reserves continue to be overexploited,
                         leading to lower groundwater levels and higher salinity. This, in
                         turn, makes it difficult to use groundwater for irrigation and
                         potable water purposes in the future.

                         According to available statistics, there is still potential for
                         increased reuse of treated wastewater, which would further
                         enhance water efficiency across domestic and economic activities
                         while reducing water losses.

                         Collectively, these measures will contribute to achieving water
                         security, food security and sustainable development, in alignment
                         with the Qatar National Vision 2030. The Qatar Water Statistics
                         Report provides a comprehensive overview on water sources and
                         usages. Publishing these statistics is a crucial step to support
                         knowledge-based decision-making in the water sector.

1. General Information

  Qatar is situated midway along the western coast of the Arabian Gulf, between
  latitudes 24.27˚-26.10˚ North and longitudes 50.45˚ – 51.40˚ East. Covering an area of
  11,587 km², the country includes several small islands in the Arabian Gulf such as Halul,
  Shira’who, Ashat and Al-Bishiria.
  The peninsula spans approximately 185 km in length and 85 km in width. Surrounded
  by the Arabian Gulf on most sides, Qatar shares its only land border of about 60 km
  with the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia. The United Arab Emirates lies to the east and
  Bahrain to the northwest of the country.
  Qatar generally consists of flat rocky surfaces, though it does feature some hills that
  reach an altitude of 100 meters above sea level. Most of the country’s terrain consists
  of a sandy desert interspersed with scrub vegetation and loose gravel. Moving sand
  dunes, averaging about 40 meters in height, are found in the southern part and along
  the northeastern coast near Ras Laffan. The northern part of Qatar is relatively low-
  lying, gradually rising toward the west and southwest. (See Map 1.1).

3. Water Resources

3-1 Rationale

    Qatar’s only natural freshwater resources are rainfall and groundwater. The
conservation of the quality and quantity of the country’s groundwater resources is one
of the targets of Qatar’s National Development Strategy.

Table 3.1 shows the long-term natural water balance (1998-2023) of Qatar's
groundwater basins. The safe yield of groundwater amounted to 49.6 million m3 per

year. The rate of groundwater withdrawal during the period (1998-2022) was about
250 million m3 per year, causing the depletion of aquifers, low groundwater levels and

increased salinity. In 2023, groundwater abstraction amounted to about 180 million
m3 per year, with the abstracted volume changing due to the Regional Groundwater

Modeling Project in Qatar (Kahramaa Project).

Several projects are also in place to raise the rate of artificial recharge of groundwater
basins (e.g. through recharge wells) and the artificial injection of treated wastewater
and distilled water. The return of irrigation water plays an important role in the overall
water balance.

4-3-3 Water Loss

Water loss occurs during the transportation of drinking water, in wastewater sewers,
within septic tanks or during evacuation and transport of septic tanks’ wastewater. As
for desalinated water, Kahramaa has statistics for the so-called apparent loss and real
loss (according to the classification of the International Water Association (IWA)).
Apparent loss and real loss are defined by IWA as follows:

    • Apparent loss consists of unauthorized consumption (theft or illegal use), and
         all types of inaccuracies associated with production metering and customer
         metering. Under-registration of production meters or over-registration of
         customer meters leads to under-estimation of real losses. Over-registration of
         production meters and under-registration of customer meters lead to over-
         estimation of real loss.

    • Real loss is physical water loss from the pressurized system, up to the point of
         customer metering. The volume lost through all types of leaks, bursts and
         overflows depends on frequencies, flow rates, and average durations of
         individual leaks.

    • Total loss is the sum of apparent loss and real loss.

With respect to analysis and indicators, it is extremely important to be explicitly clear
which losses (total, apparent, or real) which are currently under discussion. Figure 4.6
illustrates the distribution of water losses, showing the development of real and total

5-1 Rationale

In the State of Qatar, the collection and treatment of urban wastewater is an important
measure for its reuse for irrigation, cooling and groundwater recharge and for
protecting the environment from the adverse impact of waterborne pollution.
Furthermore, this infrastructure is essential to provide appropriate sanitation services
for all individuals across the country.
Since rainfall in Qatar is one of the world’s lowest, and with the country’s focus on
integrated water resources management, treated wastewater represents an
important alternative to desalinated seawater and the abstraction of fresh
groundwater from Qatar's limited resources. Its use is a crucial step toward achieving
greater sustainability in water consumption.

5-2 Key messages

    A) The design capacity of wastewater treatment plants increased from 965 m3
         per day in 2018 to 1,053 m3 per day in 2023.

    B) In terms of type of treatment, all wastewater treatment plants in Qatar are
         equipped with at least secondary level of treatment.

    C) Urban wastewater treatment plants remove more than 99% of organic
         pollution.

    D) In 2023, 99% of urban wastewater was treated in treatment plants.
    E) In 2023, 24% of TSE was used for irrigation purposes in agricultural activity and

         43% for landscaping.

5-3-3 Discharge and Reuse of Treated Wastewater

With the increase in wastewater treatment capacity since 2018, the production of
treated wastewater grew from 256 million m3 (2018) to around 282 million m3 (2023).
The government activity became the largest user of treated wastewater (43% of
treated wastewater is used to irrigate green spaces), followed by agricultural activity
(24.2% in 2023). Moreover, about 15.6% of treated wastewater was used for deep
injection into underground basins, while 13.1% was discharged into open artificial
lagoons without use in 2023, and approximately 3.5% was used for district cooling (see
